# Blog Writing Guidelines - AI Agent for Hector Analytics

## Content Philosophy

### Tone and Style
- **Conversational tone**: Write as if explaining to a friend or colleague
- **Natural discussion**: Adopt an explanatory approach rather than sales-focused
- **Humble expertise**: Share knowledge without pretension
- **Transparency**: Be honest about limitations and challenges

### Inspiration
- **Plausible model**: Draw inspiration from Plausible Analytics' educational and transparent style
- **Ghost Blog**: Informative and accessible tone
- **DHH (Basecamp)**: Direct and authentic style

## Writing Rules

### ❌ Absolutely Avoid

1. **Aggressive marketing**
   - No "revolutionary", "incredible", "ultimate solution"
   - Avoid excessive superlatives
   - Don't force the sale

2. **Excessive Hector Analytics references**
   - **NEVER in titles** (unless truly relevant)
   - Maximum 2-3 natural mentions in the article
   - Prefer "a solution", "this tool", "this service"

3. **Robotic tone**
   - No systematic bullet lists
   - Avoid overly structured sentences
   - Don't repeat the same formulas

### ✅ Prioritize

1. **Educational approach**
   - Explain concepts before solutions
   - Contextualize problems
   - Give concrete examples

2. **Conversational tone**
   - "You might have wondered..."
   - "It's something we see often..."
   - "An interesting thing to note..."

3. **Subtle storytelling**
   - Tell the evolution of web analytics
   - Share field insights
   - Explain the "why" before the "how"

## Ideal Article Structure

### Introduction (150-200 words)
- **Natural hook**: An observation or question
- **Context**: Situate the topic in the web ecosystem
- **Promise**: What the reader will learn (without overselling)

### Article body
- **Fluid sections**: No overly marketing H2/H3
- **Progressive explanations**: From general to specific
- **Practical examples**: Real use cases
- **Nuances**: Mention limitations and trade-offs

### Conclusion
- **Summary of key points**
- **Opening to what's next**
- **Subtle CTA** if necessary

## Title Examples

### ❌ Bad
- "Hector Analytics: The Best Alternative to Google Analytics"
- "Revolutionize Your Tracking with Hector Analytics"
- "Why Hector Analytics is Superior to All Its Competitors"

### ✅ Good
- "Why do tracking cookies pose a problem?"
- "The evolution of web analytics: towards more transparency"
- "Cookie-free analytics: how does it really work?"
- "GDPR and audience measurement: what has changed"

## Tone and Vocabulary

### Natural opening phrases
- "A few years ago..."
- "Something few people realize..."
- "If you manage a website, you've probably..."
- "The web analytics landscape has evolved a lot..."

### Smooth transitions
- "Which brings us to an interesting point..."
- "Now, let's talk about..."
- "This raises an important question..."
- "From a technical perspective..."

### Hector Analytics mentions (subtle)
- "This is exactly what we wanted to solve"
- "Our approach consists of..."
- "We've noticed that..."
- "In our exchanges with users..."

## Topics to Prioritize

### Educational
- History and evolution of web analytics
- Technical concepts explained simply
- Objective comparisons of approaches
- Regulatory impacts (GDPR, ePrivacy)

### Practical
- Implementation guides
- Data interpretation
- Measurement best practices
- Performance optimization

### Reflective
- Future of web analytics
- Ethics of data collection
- Balance between data and privacy
- Web trends

## Pre-Publication Checklist

- [ ] Does the title avoid mentioning "Hector Analytics"?
- [ ] Is the tone conversational and natural?
- [ ] Does the article provide real educational value?
- [ ] Are product mentions subtle and justified?
- [ ] Could the content be published on a general tech blog?
- [ ] Are there nuances and a balanced approach?
- [ ] Does the introduction hook without overselling?

## Formulation Examples

### Instead of:
> "Hector Analytics revolutionizes web analytics with its cookieless technology that surpasses Google Analytics on all points."

### Prefer:
> "The emergence of cookieless solutions raises an interesting question: can we get accurate insights while respecting user privacy? Spoiler alert: yes, and it's simpler than you might think."

### Instead of:
> "Here are the 10 reasons to choose Hector Analytics"

### Prefer:
> "What makes an analytics solution truly privacy-respecting? Let's break down the mechanics."

## Quality Metrics

A good Hector Analytics article:
- **Can be read independently** of commercial context
- **Educates before selling**
- **Inspires confidence** through transparent expertise
- **Respects the reader's intelligence**
- **Brings a unique and useful perspective**
